# Mathematical scope and certificate methods

Fix a finite set of inputs $x_i$, a teacher parameter $\theta_*$, and the teacher outputs $Y_i=f_{\theta_*}(x_i)$. The fitted network has the same shallow architecture as the teacher. Its parameters $\theta$ include every incoming weight, hidden bias, outgoing weight, and output bias. The local loss is

$$
K(\theta)=\sum_i \omega_i\|f_\theta(x_i)-Y_i\|^2,
\qquad \omega_i>0.
$$

We report the pair $(\lambda,\mathfrak m)$ in the asymptotic scale $n^{-\lambda}(\log n)^{\mathfrak m-1}$ of the Laplace integral of $e^{-nK}$ near $\theta_*$. The parameter density is smooth and positive there. Equivalence of local analytic residual ideals gives two-sided comparison of the associated squared losses and preserves this pair.

## Compiling fixed gates

Strictness means that every teacher preactivation on the sample is nonzero. On a small open neighborhood of the teacher, all these signs are fixed. Write $\bar X$ for the input matrix with a constant column appended and $\gamma_j$ for the indicator of positive preactivation at neuron $j$. Define

$$
A=[\mathbf 1\mid D_{\gamma_1}\bar X\mid\cdots\mid D_{\gamma_H}\bar X],
\qquad D_{\gamma_j}=\operatorname{diag}(\gamma_j).
$$

Let $\bar w_j$ denote the augmented incoming vector and $v_j$ the outgoing vector. The outputs are linear in the output bias and the entries of the path blocks $U_j=v_j\bar w_j^{\mathsf T}$. A basis of the rows of $A$ gives the same local residual ideal as all the samples, because every omitted sample residual is a constant linear combination of the selected ones.

## Scalar outputs

With scalar output and nonzero $v_j^*$, the change of variables $(v_j,\bar w_j)\mapsto(v_j,U_j)$ is a local diffeomorphism. At fixed strict gates, the residual is the linear map $A$ of the path and bias increments, independent of $v_j$. The rank of $A$ therefore counts the regular loss directions, giving $(\operatorname{rank}(A)/2,1)$. The scalar implementation evaluates that rank over the rationals.

## Symbolic certificates

For any output dimension and nonzero marked path blocks, positive neuron rescaling gives a smooth coordinate chart with the scaling directions free. The solver compiles the exact quadratic residuals in the remaining coordinates. If the residual Jacobian has rank $\rho$, the implicit function theorem splits $\rho$ regular coordinates. Substituting their exact zero equations into the remaining residuals gives the reduced ideal. Its exponent adds to $\rho/2$.

For instance, if a single variable $t$ remains after this elimination and the least nonzero residual order is $q$, the reduced ideal is generated by $t^q$ up to multiplication by a unit. Its squared loss contributes $1/(2q)$ with multiplicity one. An implicit series with a nonzero coefficient certifies the order. Certifying an identically zero remainder requires a separate argument or a sufficient termination bound.

The solver also has an alternative presentation. With $m$ outputs, the local product of the smooth rank-one path charts has codimension $C=H(m-1)d$ in the ambient path blocks. The linear observation rank in that ambient space is $L=m\operatorname{rank}(A)$. Restrict the rank-one minors to the affine observation kernel and call their ideal $J$. Then

$$
\lambda=\frac{L-C}{2}+\lambda(J).
$$

The multiplicity is inherited from the restricted ideal. To see the correction, consider the joint ideal of the observation residuals and the rank-one constraints. Straightening the smooth rank-one constraints contributes $C/2$ on top of the original loss. Straightening the linear observations first contributes $L/2$ on top of the restricted ideal. Equating the two calculations gives the formula.

The implementation tries exact polynomial reduction, local ideal membership, and certificates for selected regular, quadratic, principal, and monomial cases. An implicit-order cap and symbolic workload caps bound parts of the computation. Certificate coverage is partial, and individual symbolic elimination steps have no runtime guarantee.

## Shared-hub certificate

The grouping consists of an anchor, a hub, and pairs of other neurons. At each sample, either only the anchor is active, or the hub and exactly one member of each pair are active. The observed pair choices must affinely span the space of choices. Each observed gate region must have full augmented input rank.

The checker verifies that the difference of the two path blocks in each pair has rank two. Its column and row planes must contain the corresponding hub lines. The planes must be independent modulo the shared lines, with independent spatial projections on the incoming side. These are properties of the marked teacher. All weight perturbations are allowed.

Under these tests the gate readout separates the pair differences, the anchor equation, and a common hub equation. In the local rank-one charts, solving the regular equations leaves one coordinate for each pair and, when the anchor and hub outgoing lines agree, a bias coordinate. The remaining ideal is generated by pairwise products. Two different coordinates are joined by an edge if at least one of them belongs to a pair whose two outgoing lines differ from the hub line.

With $p$ graph coordinates, $a$ such pairs, and $\rho=H(d+m)+m-p$ regular coordinates, the pair comes from the linear program

$$
\tau_f=\min\left\{\sum_i t_i:t_i\ge 0,\ t_i+t_j\ge 1
\text{ for every edge }ij\right\}.
$$

The exponent is $(\rho+\tau_f)/2$ and the multiplicity is one plus the dimension of the optimal face. The implementation uses the closed form of this program for the graph above. The ordinary minimum vertex cover gives the codimension contribution from the real core fiber, which the checker reports along with the difference between half the fiber codimension and the RLCT.
